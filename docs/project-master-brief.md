B2. Light and colour [OBS]
• Base light is cold blue-grey from windows; warm light is local (wall sconces, candles) (3 s, 60 s). Large areas fall into near-black between lights (27 s, 48 s).
• Palette: weathered grey-brown stone, dark wood, deep red cloth, gold trim, one cool accent. Boss effects add orange fire and violet magic on top.
B3. Interpretation for Black Sigil [INT]
• The strongest transferable ideas are composition ones: a long axis with a destination (dais, bright doorway), repeated vertical rhythm, and light used in small warm pools against cool dark.
• This footage is interior only. It tells us nothing about hills, slopes, grass, trees, rock outcrops, or the approach silhouette of a castle. Those need the environment images or other footage.
B4. Batch 2 — exterior, approach and distant view [OBS]
• Approach as a stair: a very wide, long flight of dark grey steps leads up to a cathedral facade, edged by autumn-coloured trees and pale dead thorn shrubs (video 2, 102–156 s; image B2-3). The destination stays visible above the player the whole way.

O. Input system and Controls menu
One registry, one listener. Every key the game reads is an action in ReplicatedStorage/Shared/Input/ActionDefs (id, label, category, context Gameplay/Menu/Always, default, gamepad default, Essential, KeyboardOnly, Available, Hold). Adding a weapon skill, spell or item action is one Register call; it appears in the Controls menu automatically.
Pieces
• Shared/Input/InputService (client): the only place that connects to UserInputService. Public API: OnPressed/OnReleased(action), IsDown, GetLabel (keyboard or gamepad glyph by last input device), Rebind (returns conflict info, supports swap), ResetToDefaults, Export/Import, PushEscape, SetMenuOpen, Block. Ignores game-processed input and typing in a TextBox. It also writes the player's W/A/S/D keys into the engine's MoveAction, disables the engine jump, and clears camera keys that would collide.
• ServerScriptService/Systems/SettingsService: DataStore BlackSigilSettings, key u_<UserId>. Validates every set against the registry, rate-limits requests, saves 6 s after the last change (retry 20 s), flushes on PlayerRemoving and BindToClose. A failing DataStore never stops play.
• StarterPlayerScripts/Input/BindingSync: loads on join, sends changes (debounced 1.5 s).
• StarterPlayerScripts/Input/PromptBinder: gives every standard ProximityPrompt the Interact key before it is shown. The Sigil's custom prompt reads InputService directly.
• Shared/UI/Menu/ControlsPanel: the Controls tab of the existing menu. Listening state ("Press any key"), conflict message naming the action that already uses the input with Swap or Cancel, Escape and a Cancel button, two-step Restore Defaults, and not-yet-available actions are greyed with a note.
Rules: essential actions can never be left unassigned; actions conflict when their contexts overlap (Always overlaps everything); arrow keys are reserved for the camera; Escape is not assignable.
Default bindings: W/S/A/D move, Left Shift sprint, Left Alt slow-walk, Space dodge (with sprint: running jump-roll, the only jump), LMB light, RMB heavy/charge, MMB lock-on*, Left Ctrl weapon skill*, X draw/sheathe, R health consumable*, F mana consumable*, 1 switch main-hand*, 2 switch offhand*, G cast spell*, E interact, I menu, H show vitals, Q/E menu tabs. (* action registered, feature not built yet)

T. Steel surface pass (rev 17)
Problem: plain grey Metal read as grey plastic. The scene's green colour correction tinted all metal, and near the campfire the very saturated fire light turned it orange or tan.
Texture pipeline (repeat for every armor piece):
1. In Blender, UV-unwrap the piece (Smart UV Project) and export the FBX again.
2. Bake three maps from its shape in Cycles: ambient occlusion (distance 0.35, with every other object hidden from render so the bake is clean), an edge map (pointiness) and an up-facing map (world normal Z).
3. Compose the maps in Python:
    ◦ Colour: dark forged steel (about 0.24 / 0.25 / 0.27), darker in the recesses, a baked polished sheen on upward curves, bright worn edges and faint scratches. It reads as metal even on low graphics, where reflections are off.
    ◦ Roughness: about 0.24, rougher in the recesses.
    ◦ Metalness: about 0.85.
4. Blender serves the textures folder at http://127.0.0.1:8765 (local only, while Blender is open). Studio's image upload only accepts http URLs, so this is how the PNGs are uploaded.
5. Studio: Import 3D (Upload to Roblox), then a SurfaceAppearance named Steel on the template with the three maps. The template MeshPart's Color is white and its Material is Metal.

Z. Roll cancel and input buffering (rev 23)
User request: be able to roll right after a swing, with no delay. Choices: a roll may cancel the swing once the blade has passed, and early presses are buffered. The "no dead time elsewhere" options were left to my call, so I added attacking out of the end of a roll and chain buffering, but not a roll out of a flinch (DS3 does not allow it).
Rules (client MovementController.rollCancelOpen, mirrored on the server in WeaponService.requestRoll):
• Light attack: a roll may start once the damage window is over (wind-up + active). The wind-up and the hit stay committed.
• Heavy: a roll may start while charging, or once the strike's damage window is over.
• Roll, backstep or land-roll: the next roll or an attack may start in the last 20% of the roll (ROLL_TAIL 0.8).
• Buffering: a dodge or light attack pressed before it is allowed is remembered for 0.35 s (BUFFER) and fires the moment it becomes possible. Dodge wins if both are buffered.
• Server: CANCEL_SLACK of 0.1 s accepts a roll slightly before the server's own window edge, to cover network jitter. Light and heavy requests in the roll's tail end the roll and start the swing.
The backups are in ServerStorage/Backups/RollCancel_v1.
Tested in Play with real input (timestamps from the client log):
• Roll during recovery: pressed 0.62 s into a light attack, the roll started 0.06 s later. Before, it had to wait for the whole swing (0.79 s).
• Roll during the wind-up: pressed 0.23 s in, it was held and fired at 0.46 s, the moment the blade passed. The swing never reached Recovery.
• Roll then roll: a second press early in the first roll fired at the roll's tail, and stamina was spent twice.
• Combo buffer: a second light attack pressed 0.42 s in (before the 0.46 s chain window) was buffered, and Light 2 started.
• Attack out of a roll: a light attack pressed 0.55 s into a 0.6 s roll; the server went straight from Rolling to Attacking.
To send an attack and a roll in one input sequence, Light Attack was temporarily bound to K during testing. The original LMB binding was restored and checked afterward.
Not verified: feel on a real network with higher latency; gamepad input.
AA. Lock-on (rev 24)
User choices: switch targets with a mouse flick; DS3 strafing, with sprint breaking the facing; a small white dot marker; and the lock breaks on distance, line of sight or death, retargeting the nearest enemy.
Files:
• StarterPlayerScripts/Combat/LockOn (new).
• Shared/Combat/LockOnState (new, client-shared state).
• MovementController: lockDir(); facing and attack direction while locked.
• SliceConfig.LockOn holds the tunables.
• ActionDefs: LockOn is now available on middle mouse (gamepad R3).
• The backups are in ServerStorage/Backups/LockOn_v1.
Behaviour:
• Press LockOn: locks the enemy most in front of the camera within 32 studs and in sight. With nothing to lock, the camera swings behind the knight. Pressing again releases.
• Switching: a sideways mouse flick (70 px within 0.18 s; mouse Delta when the cursor is locked, otherwise the change in cursor position) moves the lock to the nearest enemy on that side of the screen, with a 0.35 s cooldown. On gamepad, the right stick pushed sideways.
• Camera: sits behind the knight, offset 2.4 studs to the right so the knight does not hide the target, and looks between the two. It runs right after the default camera, so releasing the lock does not snap.
• Facing: while locked and not sprinting, the knight keeps turning to face the target (walk turn rate x1.4), so walking circles and strafes around it. Every attack goes at the target. Rolls go in the direction pressed. Sprinting turns with the run.
• Breaking: beyond 45 studs, more than 1 s out of sight (other bodies never block the line), or when the target dies, in which case the lock jumps to another enemy within 22 studs.
• Marker: a small ivory dot with a soft halo, drawn in screen space at the target's chest. A BillboardGui version did not render in captures, so it was replaced.
Tested in Play with real input:
• The lock acquired the enemy in front, and the knight turned to face it (facing · direction = 1.000).
• A mouse flick to the right switched to the other enemy; the camera followed and the knight faced the new target.
• Holding D for 1.5 s circled the target while still facing it (1.000).
• When the target left (a real Ghoul walked home), the lock broke.
• Killing the target retargeted to the remaining dummy.
• No script errors.
Testing used L as a temporary lock key, because middle mouse cannot be simulated; the binding was restored to middle mouse afterward. Test enemies were brain-less clones of a Ghoul.
Caveats:
• One earlier retarget attempt did not jump to the second dummy, and the cause was not pinned down. The later run worked, and the line-of-sight rule was loosened afterward so bodies cannot block it.
• Strafing uses the forward walk animation, so there are no sideways steps yet.
• Sprint breaking the facing, attacking toward the target and gamepad switching are untested.
